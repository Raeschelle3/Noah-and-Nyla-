TARGET DEFINITION

ILLUSTRATIVE CLASS ECONOMICS
Option A: 20 paid seats at $1,000 = $20,000 gross sales per cohort.
Option B: 16 paid seats at $1,250 = $20,000 gross sales per cohort.
Prices and demand are unvalidated. These examples assume every seat pays the full price, with no discounts or refunds. Neither example establishes that the delivery schedule can support a new cohort each month.
The existing $17 book would require 1,177 full price sales to exceed $20,000 gross ($20,009), before fees or refunds. Book sales are a supplementary path until actual conversion supports a larger assumption.

CASH IS NOT COMPLETED DELIVERY
Track contracted sales, cash collected, refunds, delivery obligations, and revenue separately. A January class paid for in December creates cash in December and a future delivery obligation. Do not count the same sale again when the class is delivered. Formal accounting treatment should be confirmed with the business's accountant. A multiweek cohort spanning months needs consistent reporting rather than attributing its entire value to whichever month improves the target.

COSTS TO ESTABLISH BEFORE SETTING A FINAL PRICE
Obtain actual amounts for PMI authorization or partner arrangements, required materials and licenses, instructor preparation and delivery time, guest instructors, platform subscriptions, payment processing, insurance where applicable, marketing, customer support, refunds, and applicable taxes. No amounts are assumed here.
For each cohort calculate: actual net sales less direct delivery costs = cohort contribution. Then subtract allocated operating expenses to estimate operating profit. Personal withdrawals require a separate cash review.

CASH CONTROLS
1. Do not spend expected class revenue before enrollment and delivery costs are known.
2. Keep business activity identifiable and separate from personal spending in the records.
3. Match every order to its payment status, amount collected, applicable fees, refunds, and product or cohort.
4. Preserve funds needed for promised delivery and potential refunds. Set the amount from actual obligations, rather than an invented blanket percentage.
5. Use a written cancellation and refund policy before accepting class payments.
6. Confirm the training approval path, permitted marketing claims, dates, capacity, and checkout identity before opening enrollment.
7. Reconcile provider statements with bank deposits weekly. A payment processor balance is not necessarily available cash.
