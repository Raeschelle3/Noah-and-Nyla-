**For:** anyone 45+ who has been married, divorced or widowed. Rae leans toward Black love, and the channel is for everybody.

**Format:** narrated story Shorts with real scenes (60–90 seconds). No talking characters. Always visuals, never words on a color background alone.

## The rule: mirror the top video
1. **Tell it from inside the pain.** The viewer should think "that's me." Never judge someone else's choices (that's why the age-gap story flopped).
2. **One person, one specific moment.** "His family stopped calling." "He couldn't name one thing he liked." Specific beats general.
3. **Title = the moment + the question they've asked themselves.**
4. **Scenes, not text.** 4–6 scenes with gentle motion, captions, narration and music.
5. **End with a question for the viewer.** Every few episodes: "Tell me your age and your city."
6. **Use themes from comments, never people.** No names, photos or exact quotes from anyone's comments.

## Episode shape (60–90 seconds)
| Time | Beat | Scene |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | Hook: the painful moment, spoken and on screen | The character in that moment |
| 3–20s | Who they are, what life used to look like | A memory: the full table, the family photo |
| 20–45s | The moment it hit | The quiet house, the phone that doesn't ring |
| 45–70s | The turn: one small, brave choice | They do one thing for themselves |
| 70–90s | The question to the viewer | Character looks up; end card |

## Fri Nov 27: The Holiday's Over. Everyone Went Home. Now What?
**Title:** `The Holiday's Over. Everyone Went Home. Now What? #Shorts`
**Description:** `The plates are washed. The house is quiet again. If the day after the holiday is the hardest part, you're not alone. Tell me: what do you do when everyone goes home? #SecondChapterLove #Divorced #Widowed #Over50`

**Narration (about 75 seconds)**
> The last car pulled out of the driveway at 9:14.
> Carla stood at the door and waved until the taillights were gone.
> For twenty-two years, this was the part she liked best. The kids gone, the dishes in the sink, and him on the couch saying, "We did good, babe."
> This year, there's no one on the couch.
> She washes every plate by hand. Slowly. Because when the kitchen is clean, the night is over, and then it's just her.
> Nobody warns you about the day after. Everyone checks on you before the holiday. Nobody calls after.
> So tonight, Carla does something new. She makes herself a plate of leftovers, puts it on the good china, lights the candle, and sits in his chair.
> Not to replace him. To remind herself: this is still her house. This is still her life.
> What do you do when everyone goes home?

**Runway scenes (9:16, gentle motion)**
1. *Cinematic, night. A short, curvy, stylish woman about 48 with long straight hair stands in the doorway of a warm suburban home, waving as car taillights disappear down the driveway. Porch light, cold air, her breath visible.*
2. *Warm flashback tone. A full, happy holiday kitchen: grown kids laughing, a handsome man in his 50s on the couch smiling at her. Soft glow, slightly dreamy.*
3. *The same kitchen now, quiet. She washes a plate by hand at the sink, alone, looking out the dark window. Single pendant light.*
4. *Close-up: she places leftovers on fine china, lights one candle at the dining table.*
5. *She sits in the big armchair at the head of the table, takes a breath, and gives a small, brave smile. Candlelight on her face.*

## Mon Nov 30: He Lost His Wife. Is He Ready to Love Again?
**Title:** `He Lost His Wife. Is He Ready to Love Again? #Shorts`
**Description:** `Three years later, he still sets out two mugs. Grief doesn't have a deadline, and love doesn't have an expiration date. When did you know you were ready? #SecondChapterLove #Widower #Over50`

**Narration (about 80 seconds)**
> Marcus still sets out two coffee mugs every morning. Three years, and his hands haven't learned.
> Thirty-one years with Diane. She picked his ties. She knew he'd say yes to dessert before he did.
> His friends say, "Man, it's time. Get back out there."
> His sister keeps sending him pictures of a woman from her church.
> But every time he thinks about sitting across from someone new, it feels like cheating on a woman who isn't here.
> Here's what nobody tells widowers: being ready isn't forgetting her. It's realizing she'd want you to live.
> This morning, Marcus puts the second mug back in the cabinet.
> Not because he's over her. Because he's starting to believe there might be room for both: the love he had, and the love that's still out there.
> If you've lost your spouse, how did you know you were ready? Or are you still waiting?

**Runway scenes**
1. *Early morning kitchen. A tall, handsome Black man about 58 with a salt-and-pepper beard, in a gray sweater, pours coffee and sets out two mugs out of habit. Soft window light.*
2. *Warm memory: the same man, younger, laughing with his wife as she straightens his tie in front of a mirror.*
3. *He sits on the edge of the bed holding his phone, looking at a photo his sister sent, unsure.*
4. *Close-up of his hand placing the second mug back in the cabinet and gently closing the door.*
5. *He stands on the porch with one mug, looking out at a sunrise, a hint of a smile.*

## Wed Dec 2: Are We Too Hard on Each Other?
**Title:** `Are We Too Hard on Each Other? #Shorts`
**Description:** `He says the good women are taken. She says the good men are gone. What if they're both standing in the same room, guarded? #SecondChapterLove #BlackLove #Over50`

**Narration (about 75 seconds)**
> Denise walked into the party and saw him before he saw her. Tall. Bald. Nice shoes. Good smile.
> And in two seconds, her mind did what it always does. "He's probably got three women. He's probably still married. He's probably just like the last one."
> Across the room, Andre saw her too. Beautiful. Put together. And his mind said: "She's out of my league. She's going to want a list. She's been hurt, and she's going to make me pay for it."
> Two people. Both hurt. Both guarded. Both standing ten feet apart, protecting themselves from something that hasn't even happened.
> We say there are no good ones left. But sometimes the good ones are right there, and our past is doing all the talking.
> Denise took a breath and walked over anyway. "Hi. I'm Denise."
> Have we been through so much that we can't see each other anymore?

**Runway scenes**
1. *Elegant holiday party, warm lights. A beautiful dark-skinned Black woman about 52 with natural curls, gold hoops and a fitted dress walks in and notices someone across the room.*
2. *Across the room, a fit bald Black man about 54 in a sharp blazer holds a drink and glances at her, then looks away, guarded.*
3. *Split screen: both of them looking down, arms folded, lost in thought.*
4. *She sets down her glass, takes a breath and walks across the room.*
5. *Close-up: she offers her hand with a warm smile; he smiles back.*
